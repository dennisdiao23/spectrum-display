A seamless wall for the living room — cinema scale, lights on, no projector cave.

The wall is the picture

A 75-inch TV looks small on a long sofa wall. This is a fine-pitch COB wall built to the room: one picture, no bezel grid, bright enough that you do not have to draw the shades.

Sit eight feet away and it should still look like a television, not a billboard.

Made to live with, not just to watch

The cabinets we use for homes are COB — the pixels sit under a sealed face.

Dust the surface

You can dust the surface. Exposed-lamp walls cannot take a cloth.

Lower power

Lower power than a typical older LED wall of the same size.

Quiet in the room

Quiet in the room. No rack that belongs on a concert stage.

Front service

A module can come off from the front if something ever needs service.

Sized to your wall, not to a TV box

Most living rooms land in one of these classes. We confirm pitch from how close the sofa sits.

  • About 108″ — a big family-room picture
  • About 135″ — the wall most people mean when they say “replace the TV”
  • About 162″ — great room or dedicated theater

Custom widths and 21:9 cinema shapes are fine when the wall is wider than it is tall.

We will ask for a photo of the wall, the width in inches, and how far you sit. Then we send a plan — size, finish, and what it takes to hang.

Living room or a dark theater — both work

Lights stay on. Sports in the afternoon still look like sports.
A dedicated media room can go darker and closer, with a tighter pitch.

Power is planned so it is not a snake of cables on the baseboard. HDMI from the rack or the cabinet — it should feel like a television to the family, not like a venue.

We plan it. A local installer hangs it.

You are not expected to unbox cabinets in the living room.

  1. Send the room (photo + wall width + seating distance).
  2. We spec the wall and a simple living-room setup.
  3. A professional installer — yours or one we introduce — mounts it and hands you a remote.

Spare modules stay with the house so a pixel issue is a service call, not a construction project.
